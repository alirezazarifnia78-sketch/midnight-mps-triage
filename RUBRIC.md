# Triage rubric

Each criterion scores **0** (missing), **1** (partly there) or **2** (strong). The maximum is **14**.

| # | Criterion | Question I ask |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Completeness | Are all MPS template sections filled in with real content, not placeholders? |
| 2 | Problem, not solution | Does it describe the gap without prescribing the fix? Design belongs in a MIP. |
| 3 | Evidence | Is there data, measurement or real users behind it, or only opinion? |
| 4 | Who is affected | Is it clear which builders or users are hurt, and how badly? |
| 5 | Dependencies declared | Are related MPSs and MIPs linked in the header, not only mentioned in the text? |
| 6 | Overlap handled | Does it acknowledge MPSs or MIPs that cover similar ground? |
| 7 | Fit and urgency | Does it match current priorities, and is something blocked on it now? |

## Gates (outside the score)

Some things shouldn't be traded off against a score:

- **Privacy or security impact:** anything that could weaken privacy guarantees goes to security review, whatever its score.
- **Tokens, compliance or legal exposure:** goes to the relevant reviewers before moving forward.

## How I read the score

- **11–14:** ready for the product team to discuss.
- **7–10:** worth pursuing; send specific fixes to the author first.
- **0–6:** send back with clear guidance (often to split the problem from a proposed solution).

The score is a starting point for discussion, not a decision. Its job is to make my assumptions visible so authors and reviewers can challenge them.
